How these numbers are worked out

Everything on this page is worked out from the CSV each time the page opens. Only the billing rows are read; the name, address and account lines at the top of the export are ignored. The file is never uploaded. Billing periods include both their first and last day.

A bill counts toward the month that holds the middle of its billing period. The year-over-year figures compare the latest year with the year before, using only months that have a bill in both years. The monthly chart draws every year with at least two bills, up to the six most recent.

Summer vs winter compares average kWh per day in the latest summer with all three months billed (June–August) against the winter just before it (December–February). Using kWh per day keeps short or long billing periods from skewing the comparison.

The average price per kWh is total cost divided by total kWh, so it includes fixed monthly charges and taxes, not only the per-kWh rate.
